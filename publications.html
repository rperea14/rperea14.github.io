Neurobiology of Aging • 2019

Synergism between fornix microstructure and beta amyloid accelerates memory decline in clinically normal older adults

Perea RD, Rabin JS, Buckley RF, Johnson KA, Sperling RA, Hedden T.
Abstract: The fornix is the primary cholinergic and subcortical efferent pathway from the hippocampus. In clinically normal older adults, we tested whether fornix diffusion microstructure interacts with cortical β-amyloid (Aβ) burden to accelerate cognitive decline over longitudinal follow-up. Findings demonstrated that microstructural fornix degradation synergistically amplified Aβ-related memory decline, offering key prognostic utility for early Alzheimer's disease detection.

Nature Neuroscience • 2018

Reduced tract integrity predicts downstream tau accumulation in amyloid positive normal older individuals

Jacobs HIL, Hedden T, Perea RD, Schultz AP, Sepulcre J, Johnson KA, et al.
Abstract: Understanding how Alzheimer's pathology propagates across connected brain networks is critical for early therapeutic intervention. Using advanced diffusion connectomics and tau-PET imaging, this study identified white matter tract degradation as an antecedent structural vulnerability facilitating the spread of tau neurofibrillary tangles from medial temporal regions into neocortex.

NeuroImage: Clinical • 2019

Connectome-derived diffusion characteristics of the fornix in Alzheimer's disease

Perea RD, Rabin JS, Schultz AP, Sperling RA, Johnson KA, Hedden T.
Abstract: Quantitative diffusion MRI tractography of the fornix in large-scale clinical cohorts (n>400). Demonstrates automated probabilistic segmentation frameworks to quantify axonal and myelin alterations in preclinical and symptomatic Alzheimer's dementia.
